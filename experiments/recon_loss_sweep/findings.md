# Findings — 20k reconstruction-loss sweep on Gemma-3-12B (L32)

Hand-written summary of the 20k dry-run sweep. The full per-token / per-bucket
tables live in `results_dryrun_20k.md` (auto-generated by `analyze.py`).

**TL;DR.** Reconstruction is *uniformly very good* (median cos = 0.996,
p10 = 0.988). The interesting structure is in the tails:

- **Worst-reconstructed real tokens** are sentence-initial capitalised words
  (`How`, `What`), proper nouns / brand names (`Android`, `ubuntu`, `VPN`),
  and short-context syntax tokens. The AV falls back to a generic
  "Technical article structure: blog post about a research paper" template
  when it can't extract specific content from the activation, and that
  template carries no content the AR can use to reconstruct.
- **Best-reconstructed tokens** split into two populations: common English
  function/content words (`been`, `large`, `connect`, `could`, `following`)
  with cos ≈ 0.998, and a degenerate "sink-token" cluster at extreme L2-norm
  (>400k) where the AV always emits the literal phrase
  "Fragmented, incoherent academic text pattern…" and the AR perfectly maps
  that phrase back to the same vector. The latter cluster reconstructs at
  cos = 1.000 but conveys ≈ no information.

## Method (brief)

- **Model:** `google/gemma-3-12b-it`, residual-stream output of layer 32
  (≈ 2/3 depth).
- **NLA pair:** `kitft/nla-gemma3-12b-L32-{av,ar}`.
- **Dataset:** `common-pile/comma_v0.1_training_dataset`, streaming + shuffle.
- **Sampling:** 20,000 random texts; for each, one random token at
  position ≥ 10 (skip the noisy template prefix per the README warning).
- **Pipeline:** extract activation → AV verbalises (sglang, max_new_tokens=200,
  temperature=0) → AR reconstructs vector → MSE / cos computed under
  unit-sphere normalisation (`MSE = 2·(1 − cos)`, range [0, 4]).
- **Run wall time:** phase 1 extract 11 min, phase 2 decode 124 min, on one
  H100 SXM 80 GB.
- **AV parse rate:** 19998 / 20000 = 99.99 %. Text quality looks accurate
  on inspection of random samples.

## 1. Overall distribution

| metric  | mean  | p10   | p50   | p90   | p99   |
|---------|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|
| cos     | 0.993 | 0.988 | 0.996 | 0.998 | 0.998 |
| mse_nrm | 0.014 | 0.005 | 0.009 | 0.024 | 0.047 |

Reading: even the *p10* of 0.988 is excellent (the README's "good decode"
benchmark is cos ≈ 0.9). The reconstruction is uniformly tight and the
distribution has a long left tail rather than wide spread.

## 2. Worst-reconstructed *tokens* (with n ≥ 5)

Top of `results_dryrun_20k.md` "Bottom 30":

| token  | n  | cos_mean | gloss |
|--------|----|----------|-------|
| `How`  | 13 | 0.616    | sentence-initial question word; the dataset has lots of forum-post titles that begin with this |
| `get`  |  5 | 0.816    | mid-word verb start, often `getX` in code or `get up/out` |
| `how`  |  6 | 0.886    | lower-case variant — still sentence-internal |
| `C`    | 10 | 0.930    | ambiguous: language name, grade, hex digit, math constant |
| `What` | 12 | 0.957    | another forum-title starter |
| `i`    | 19 | 0.957    | single letter; loop variable, pronoun, math index |

The pattern is: **short, decontextualised, semantically polysemous tokens**.
The activation at these positions has not yet accumulated the disambiguating
context, so the residual stream carries a token-identity signal but little
"about-ness". The AV needs about-ness to write a useful explanation, so it
reverts to a template, and the round-trip loses the token identity.

Below those in the table (cos 0.98-0.99) are heavy-syntax code tokens
(`}`, `");`, `}{`, `}\`, `};`, `();`, `      `), which have the same
short-and-polysemous pathology in a different domain (any code chunk).

## 3. Worst-reconstructed *individual samples* (cos < 0.2)

These are dominated by **proper nouns and sentence-starting capitalised
words at low L2-norm (≈ 36k, in the lowest decile)**:

```
cos=0.076  tok=`Android`     ‖v‖=36194  pos=254
cos=0.085  tok=`ubuntu`      ‖v‖=36404  pos=123
cos=0.101  tok=`Any`         ‖v‖=36550  pos=250
cos=0.106  tok=`VPN`         ‖v‖=36150  pos=287
cos=0.112  tok=`get`         ‖v‖=36283  pos=342
cos=0.143  tok=`How`         ‖v‖=36411  pos=391
cos=0.151  tok=`How`         ‖v‖=36411  pos=369
cos=0.154  tok=`Changing`    ‖v‖=36489  pos=232
cos=0.169  tok=`How`         ‖v‖=36402  pos=258
cos=0.182  tok=`How`         ‖v‖=36401  pos=326
```

In every case, the AV produces near-identical generic "Technical article
structure: a blog post reviewing a specific research paper, with a
structured argument about the Fibonacci sequence" prose — clearly a learned
fallback for "I can't tell what this activation is about". The AR receives
no informative text and can't reproduce the original direction.

This is consistent with NLA failing gracefully: low-information activations
get explained with low-information text, and the round-trip loss reflects
that.

## 4. Best-reconstructed tokens — two populations

**(a) Common English content/function words.** Top-30 are all space-prefixed
(word-initial in the byte-level BPE) common words: `been, large, connect,
best, always, seem, works, specific, provide, good, sure, look, could,
might, much, wrong, following, seems, input, will, between, any, fix,
But, tried, may, without, trying`. cos = 0.998. The AV emits a sensible
contextual explanation, the AR maps it back. This is the system working as
designed.

**(b) High-norm degenerate "sink" cluster.** All ten of the top-10
individual samples (cos = 1.000) are whitespace or apostrophe tokens at
high position (411-495) with extreme L2-norm (432k-477k, vs. p99 of 100k).
The AV emits the literal phrase
*"Fragmented, incoherent academic text pattern: fragmented sentences and
garbled phrasing suggest a collage or corrupted document"* — verbatim
across all ten — and the AR perfectly inverts it. These are sink-token
artefacts: the model is using these positions to carry layer-wide
bookkeeping that lives in a single learned direction; both AV and AR
have memorised that direction, so the round-trip is trivial.

These samples *should* probably be filtered before any downstream metric.
A simple rule that excludes the top ≈ 1 % by L2-norm (`vec_norm > 100k`)
would catch them — the regular content distribution lives in 25k-100k.

## 5. Cos vs. activation L2-norm

| decile | norm_range          | cos_mean |
|--------|---------------------|----------|
| 0      | 25,919 – 54,293     | 0.978    |
| 1      | 54,293 – 59,819     | 0.990    |
| 2      | 59,819 – 64,243     | 0.991    |
| ...    | ...                 | ...      |
| 9      | 90,195 – 477,188    | 0.997    |

Higher L2-norm → cleaner reconstruction, monotonically. This is the
**opposite** of the README's warning (which flagged occasional high-norm
positions as unreliable for Qwen). For Gemma-3-12B at layer 32, the
warning is inverted: low-norm activations are the ambiguous, hard-to-verbalise
ones (they're the ones that haven't accumulated much signal yet, so they're
context-poor), while high-norm activations are saturated/specialised and
verbalise reliably — except for the "sink" cluster at the very top of the
norm distribution, which verbalises trivially because it's a memorised
fallback.

## 6. Cos vs. position and seq_len

Both essentially flat once you skip the first ~10 tokens:

- Position deciles: cos_mean stays in [0.991, 0.994] across positions
  10 → 511.
- Sequence-length deciles: 0.991 → 0.993. (11k samples sit at seq_len = 512
  because of right-truncation at `--max-len 512`.)

So the reconstruction quality is **not** position- or sentence-length
dependent for Gemma-3-12B at layer 32 (past the first 10 tokens).

## 7. Suggestions for a follow-up sweep

If we re-run this at 100k or another model:

1. **Filter the high-norm "sink" cluster.** Add a `--max-vec-norm` flag to
   the extract phase and drop samples above ≈ 100k (or whatever the dataset
   p99 is). They're not informative.
2. **Stratify by token category.** Group tokens into {sentence-initial cap,
   common content word, code syntax, single char, whitespace, other} and
   report cos per category — the per-token table is informative but noisy
   (n = 5–70 per token in 20k).
3. **Capture the AV explanation when AV gives up.** The "Fragmented,
   incoherent academic text pattern" phrase appears identically in dozens of
   high-norm samples — log a flag for "explanation matches a known fallback
   template" so we can treat those samples differently downstream.
4. **The 100k sweep is mostly redundant for the headline numbers** (the
   distribution is already very tight at 20k) but would help for token-level
   tables, where 100k gives ~5x more tokens per cell.
